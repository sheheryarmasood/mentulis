Features

Tools for heavy days, and for ordinary ones.

Six things, in nine languages. Most of them free, permanently — including every part you would reach for on a bad night.

01 — Companion

Someone to talk to, any hour

An AI companion that listens and replies in your language. It is trained on evidence-based approaches, and it is clear about what it is: not a therapist, not supervised by one, and capable of being wrong.

Ten messages a day are free. Premium removes the limit.

Conversations are never stored. Each message is sent, answered and discarded — there is no history on your phone or on our servers.

02 — Mood & journal

One tap a day. A clearer picture over time.

A check-in that takes ten seconds, and a journal for everything that does not fit into five buttons. Over weeks it becomes a line you can actually read.

Free shows your last seven check-ins. Premium opens the full history.

There is no streak to break and no score to optimise. A day you skip is just a day you skipped.

03 — CBT toolkit

Techniques therapists actually use

Thought records

Catch a thought, test it, and write what you would tell a friend in the same position.

Worry journal

Separate what you can act on from what you are only carrying.

Self-checks

GAD-7 and PHQ-9 — the same screening questionnaires used in clinics. Your first is free.

Gratitude log

Three lines, no pressure to feel anything in particular. Always free.

A score is not a diagnosis. GAD-7 and PHQ-9 are screening tools. They can tell you something is worth looking at. Only a qualified clinician can tell you what it is.

04 — Breathing & grounding

For when typing is too much

Paced breathing, including the 4-7-8 pattern, and a 5-4-3-2-1 sensory exercise that asks you to look around rather than write anything.

Grounding saves nothing and asks for nothing. It stays usable when you are too overwhelmed to type — which is the point.

Try the breathing exercise on the homepage →

05 — Share with a therapist

A PDF, only when you ask

Mentulis can produce a summary of your check-ins and self-check scores as a PDF — something concrete to bring to an appointment instead of trying to remember six weeks at once.

You start it, you choose where it goes. It is the only thing in the app that sends your wellness data anywhere. Premium feature.

Coming soon

Music & sound

A small library of calming sound — rain, waves, wind, quiet room tone — with a sleep timer and seamless looping, so a short recording can run as long as you need it to. Built into the app and finished.

It stays switched off until every track in it is properly licensed for use in a paid app. Shipping audio we do not have clear rights to is not a corner we are willing to cut, so this arrives when the licensing is right — not before.

06 — Crisis resources

One tap from every screen in the app. Never behind a subscription, never behind a sign-in, never behind an onboarding flow.

The lines in the app are for Canada and the United States — 911, 988, Kids Help Phone, Hope for Wellness and 211. Elsewhere, findahelpline.com lists free, confidential helplines by country.

Nine languages

Including the two that read right to left.

The whole layout mirrors for Arabic and Urdu — navigation, chevrons, chat bubbles, the lot. Not English with translated labels.
