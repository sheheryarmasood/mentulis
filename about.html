About

Support that doesn't wait for a referral.

Most people who need mental health support are not in crisis. They are tired, stretched, stuck at three in the morning — and months away from an appointment, if one is available at all.

The gap between fine and a crisis is where most life happens.
Why Mentulis exists

Therapy is the right answer for a lot of people. It is also expensive, waitlisted, and in most of the world simply unavailable. Between the moment something starts weighing on you and the moment you can talk to a professional, there is usually nothing.

Mentulis is built for that gap. Not as a replacement for care — as something to hold on to while you wait for it, or while you decide whether you need it.

It works in nine languages because thinking about your own mind is hard enough in your first language, let alone your second. That is a capability, not a demographic: Mentulis is for anyone, anywhere, who would rather not do this in English.

What we refuse to do

Most of the design work was deciding what to leave out.

A mental health product has more ways to do harm than most software. These are the lines we hold, and they cost us money.

We never paywall safety

Crisis resources, breathing, grounding, journalling and your daily check-in are free and always will be. Premium buys depth and repetition. It never buys the thing you reach for on a bad night.

We don't store your conversations

Messages to the Companion are sent, answered and discarded. Not on your phone, not on our servers. There is no chat history — which also means there is none to leak, subpoena or sell.

We don't gamify distress

No streaks to break, no badges, no notifications engineered to pull you back. A day you skip is a day you skipped. Engagement is not the goal; it is not even a metric we would want to raise.

We don't pretend to be clinicians

The Companion says plainly that it is AI. Self-check scores are labelled as screening, never diagnosis. When something needs a professional, the app says so instead of trying to handle it.

We don't sell data, and there's little to sell

No ads, no advertising ID, no analytics SDK, no third-party sharing. Your check-ins and journal never leave your phone in the first place.

We don't invent numbers

The app shows what you actually recorded. No "your sleep is costing you 1.4 points" — we don't have that data, and fabricating insight in a mental health product is not a design flourish, it's a lie.

The company

Mentulis

Mentulis is a federally incorporated Canadian company, registered in August 2026 and based in Ontario. The app launched on Google Play in 2026, with iOS to follow.

We are early, small, and deliberately so. The product is funded to be built properly rather than grown quickly, because the failure modes of a rushed mental health app land on people who are already struggling.

Revenue comes from Premium subscriptions. Not from advertising, not from data, and not from selling insight about users to anyone — including employers who offer Mentulis to their staff.

What's next

Where we are, and what comes after.

We would rather show you the real sequence than a polished promise. Dates move; the order does not.

  1. Done

    The app, built properly

    Nine languages including right-to-left, the Companion, CBT toolkit, mood and journal, breathing and grounding, and crisis resources that are free to everyone.

  2. Now

    Closed testing on Google Play

    A small group of testers using the real app on real phones, so the first public version is one we have actually watched people use.

  3. Next

    Public launch on Android

    Open availability on Google Play, with Premium subscriptions live.

  4. After that

    iOS

    The same app on iPhone. Same features, same privacy posture, same free tier.

  5. In progress

    Music & sound

    Built and waiting. The feature is finished; it stays switched off until every track in it is properly licensed, because shipping audio we do not have clear rights to is not a corner we will cut.
